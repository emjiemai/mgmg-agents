## EMJIEM AI Agent Catalogue and Communication Plan: 40 roles for operations, finance, people, sales and service (4 Nov 2026)

A decision guide for the owner, managers and IT. It is a catalogue of recommendations, not a build spec: it sets no schedules, budgets or deadlines, and names few triggers. The recommended operating model is one authenticated staff workspace, one shared task register and separate customer channels. It lists 16 core agents and 24 optional modules that run inside a parent agent. The names describe responsibilities, so the doc says they do not need 40 subscriptions, apps or bot accounts. Start with MIYA, SCORE, CASH, PROFIT and HARI. Switch on the others only when their data, a responsible human manager and a measurable benefit are ready. Employees should see four entries: My tasks, Ask HARI, Report a problem, Request approval. MIYA routes each request. Finance and HR details appear only after identity and permission checks. Agents exchange records through APIs and the shared task queue; staff never forward messages between bots. Fixed reminders, formulas and thresholds stay ordinary automation, and AI is used only where interpretation adds value. The doc also covers: channel limits (WhatsApp 24-hour window and templates; Microsoft's ACS-based WhatsApp connection closed to new setups after 23 Oct 2026), the fields and statuses of a task record, a sample task flow, a communication rhythm, platform layers, who may see what, a 4-stage pilot and a 10-point IT acceptance checklist.

**Modules**

- **Staff workspace (single entry point)** — One authenticated place for employees with: My tasks, Ask HARI, Report a problem, Request approval. Use Microsoft Teams if company accounts and licences exist, otherwise a simple authenticated browser app. A native mobile app waits until the pilot proves a need. (triggers: Employee-initiated; data sources: Shared task register, approved knowledge, HR or finance records after permission checks; outputs and recipients: Employees: own tasks, help, updates, training, HR requests. - **01 MIYA, operations manager (core, start first)** — Turns decisions into tasks with owners, deadlines and dependencies. Follows blockers, routes handoffs and avoids repeating identical reminders. - **Task record and lifecycle** — Every task has: ID, expected result, responsible person, accepting manager, deadline, priority, status, dependencies, evidence, source links, change history. Statuses: New, Assigned, In progress, Blocked, Submitted, Accepted, Reopened, Cancelled. Sample flow: Request, Assign (link with Start / Need help / Propose new date), Assist (QUOTE plus PROFIT), Follow up, Submit (FINAL), Accept or return (SCORE counts it, THANK may suggest thanks). Uses OKR, KPI and RACI with a small number of measures. (triggers: Request, then task events; data sources: Task register; outputs and recipients: Employee, accepting manager)
- **Communication rhythm** — Start of shift: confirm key tasks and any help needed, at a time fitted to each employee's schedule. During work: updates when a task is assigned, blocked, due or changed, with routine reminders batched. End of shift: results, evidence and remaining blockers in one short update. Weekly: SCORE reviews targets, MBA prepares owner decisions. Outside shifts: only designated on-call people, only for defined urgent incidents. Staff get Uzbek and Russian. Voice transcripts must be confirmed for names, quantities, prices and dates. Notifications stay short and link to the full record. (triggers: Shift start and end, task events, weekly; data sources: Task register, schedules; outputs and recipients: Employees, managers, on-call staff)
- **Permissions matrix** — Employee: own work, common knowledge, own HR records. Department manager: department work and authorised performance data; financial detail only where explicitly allowed. Finance and HR: their assigned confidential records, with salary and supplier-cost access granted explicitly. Owner: private management and financial reporting. AI agents and IT: only the records and tools each needs; IT admin rights do not grant business data. Customer agent: public information plus verified access to that customer's case only. Permissions are enforced in the database and tools, not only in prompts. Access and changes are logged. Approval is bound to the exact action and record, and changed details need a new approval (payments, credit exceptions, unusual discounts, employment decisions, contract commitments, destructive actions). (triggers: Every access; data sources: Not specified; outputs and recipients: Not specified)

**Costs mentioned**

## AIPRO AI Procurement Agent: build specification for EMJIEM

Build AIPRO, a separate procurement service covering Garmin, Tanita, Primus, parts, detergents and LONDRY supplies. It says what to buy, what not to buy, how much, from whom and when, by combining demand, inventory, incoming supply, customer commitments, profitability, logistics and cash. Every recommendation needs evidence, a calculated quantity and a short reason. It works with MIYA, CASH, PROFIT and STOCK through shared records. A language model only reads documents and explains decisions; validated code does the calculations. No purchase order is created before a person approves the exact lines, supplier, terms and maximum spend. All thresholds are editable approved policy that AIPRO may propose changes to but never change itself. The document says its quantities, limits and stages are a proposal; real connections and values must be checked during the build (references checked 6 Nov 2026).

**Modules**

- **Order intake (Step 1 Upload, Step 2 Validate)** — The owner or an authorized employee uploads an Excel order and picks the supplier, the company and the target delivery date. Supplier codes are mapped to EMJIEM SKUs, units and currencies. Missing data, duplicate lines and unknown products are flagged. Original rows and requested quantities are kept. An ambiguous mapping gets a clarification question or HOLD. Minimum input: Line ID, SKU or supplier code, requested quantity, unit. Supplied or fetched: supplier, currency, unit price, required date, customer or project reference, Incoterm, payment terms. (triggers: Each upload; data sources: Uploaded Excel; SAP item master; supplier code map; outputs and recipients: Validated lines plus a list of exceptions, back to the uploader)
- **Source connection and snapshot (Step 3 Connect)** — Reads authorized SAP, 1C, Billz, warehouse, finance, customer-order and supplier data and saves a timestamped analysis snapshot. Each field has one authoritative source. Matching is by company, SKU, variant, unit, warehouse, currency and document line. One transaction seen in SAP, 1C and Billz is counted once. Transfers are not demand. Partial receipts reduce the open PO quantity, and PO lines are reconciled with shipments so incoming goods count once. Each source has a maximum data age; data is refreshed on upload and again before PO approval. A critical outage, an unknown SKU or an unresolved difference gives HOLD. An approved older snapshot is labelled with its date and limits. Each run records source IDs, transformations, policy version and model version. (triggers: On upload; again before PO approval; data sources: SAP B1: item and warehouse balances, SO and PO lines, partial receipts, allocations, returns, cost layers (Service Layer or an approved connector). 1C: posted purchases and sales, inventory values, AR, AP, advances, taxes, actual costs (REST/OData or controlled exports, objects restricted). Billz and CRM: retail sales, returns, promotions, out-of-stock periods, B2B deals, firm customer orders and requested dates. Warehouse: physical counts, receipt age, serials and batches, expiry, location, reservations, quarantine, damaged (reconciled with the inventory ledger first). Finance and bank: cleared customer deposits linked to orders, open AR and AP, supplier credit, available budget, dated cash commitments (a finance-approved source or CASH output; expected receipts are never cleared cash). Suppliers and logistics: quotes, currency, MOQ, pack sizes, supplier stock, lead-time history, payment milestones, Incoterms, freight, shipment ETAs, quote validity. Knowledge and signals: procurement policies, product lifecycle, manuals, contracts, seasonal calendar, FX, disruption alerts (versioned, with source date, scope and reviewer).; outputs and recipients: Stored snapshot with provenance (internal))
- **Demand and quantity engine (Step 4 Decide)** — Eight steps. (1) Segment into regular retail, intermittent parts, customer projects and new products; ABC by value or contribution, XYZ by variability, plus criticality and lifecycle. (2) Estimate demand from history, stock availability, season, promotions and verified dealer demand; flag lost sales and one-off projects; out-of-stock days are not zero demand. (3) Add firm customer quantities by due date and consume the overlapping forecast; pipeline is a separate weighted scenario; a quote or deposit alone is not extra demand. (4) Date supply day by day near term and weekly later, by SKU and warehouse, through lead time plus review interval; exclude damaged, expired and quarantined units; keep uncertain arrivals separate. (5) Safety stock from service targets, forecast uncertainty, lead-time variability and criticality. Need = F + U + SS - S - I, where S includes allocated stock only when its commitment is in F, a reservation is never deducted twice, and stock or inbound reserved for other commitments is excluded. Worked example: 10 + 30 + 5 - 12 - 8 = 25, so a request for 50 becomes ADJUST to 25 only if timing, pack size, margin and funding pass. A receipt after a customer deadline cannot cover that earlier shortage. (6) Offer transfers, substitutes and supplier rescheduling before extra buying; older usable receipts first (FIFO), earliest expiry first for dated chemicals (FEFO). (7) Apply MOQ, pack size, capacity, maximum coverage, lifecycle and order-level cash limits; zero raw need never becomes an order because of MOQ; any rounding increase is rechecked. (8) Choose a feasible mix across the whole basket: protect firm obligations and critical service stock, compare contribution, cash tied up and stockout risk, and escalate infeasible commitments. (triggers: Each analysis run; data sources: Snapshot; approved policy; outputs and recipients: Per-line decision code and quantities for the workbook)
- **Decision codes** — BUY: requested quantity justified and feasible (order the requested quantity). ADJUST: raise or lower the request with the difference explained (recalculated quantity). WAIT: existing supply or timing makes ordering now unnecessary (0 plus a review date). DO NOT ORDER: no justified need, bad economics or a policy block (0 plus a reason). HOLD: critical evidence or approval missing or conflicting (blank plus the missing evidence). (triggers: Each line; data sources: Engine output; outputs and recipients: Order_Review sheet)
- **FIFO/FEFO/LIFO and frozen (dead) stock** — FIFO dispatches the oldest suitable receipts first; FEFO the earliest expiry first. Ageing is tracked from the original receipt. LIFO may appear only as a cost comparison; the approved accounting method is kept (IAS 2 allows specific identification, FIFO or weighted average, not LIFO). Dead stock is flagged by age, demand and lifecycle with thresholds per category. AIPRO recommends clearance, transfer, return or replacement and keeps justified critical spares. Per SKU it shows sales velocity, days of cover, turnover, aged value and stockout risk. (triggers: Each run; data sources: Receipt age/cost layers, sales history, lifecycle; outputs and recipients: Stock_Demand sheet; clearance proposals)
- **Cost, cash, logistics and crisis checks** — True landed cost: net price, allocated freight, insurance, duty, handling, broker fees and nonrecoverable taxes; recoverable VAT stays out of cost but its cash timing counts; shared charges use a documented driver. Real profitability: net selling price after discounts and returns against landed cost, selling costs, financing and expected ageing losses; best sellers ranked by units, contribution and return on inventory, not revenue. Customer advance: a cleared deposit is matched to its exact contract and milestone under the approved advance requirement, funds only that contract, includes refund, tax and delivery obligations, and is never allocated twice. AR/AP and credit: overdue receivables, supplier arrears, unused approved supplier credit, due dates and currency; collection and credit are never assumed. Cash by date: supplier advance, balance, freight, duty and tax payments against opening cash and reliable receipts after existing commitments; the approved reserve is protected; a breach means reduce, split, defer or ask for a financing decision. Incoterms and route: rule, named place and version (e.g. FCA named factory Incoterms 2020); compare cost, risk transfer, loading, insurance and clearance; payment terms and title are separate fields. Arrival feasibility: production, booking, transit, customs and local delivery; air, road or sea where relevant; stale freight quotes rejected; delay ranges, damage risk and customer deadlines shown. Season SWOT and crises: base, lower-demand and disruption scenarios with approved FX, lead-time and credit assumptions; season end, product replacement, supplier concentration and demand shocks; every SWOT point tied to evidence and a change in quantity, timing or risk. (triggers: Each run; data sources: Supplier quotes and freight, 1C/SAP AR and AP, bank or CASH, FX, policy; outputs and recipients: Cash_Risk sheet; HOLD or ADJUST reasons)
- **Equipment projects and exceptions** — Primus project equipment uses contract quantities, customer funding and dated milestones, and a one-time project is never copied into recurring demand. New Garmin models account for replacement and cannibalization of old stock. A low-volume part may still be essential for a service obligation. Examples from the document: goods arriving in time give WAIT or a smaller order; an unfunded required project advance gives HOLD; obsolete excess gives DO NOT ORDER; a credible shortage before normal arrival triggers an expedite, transfer or customer-date decision. A supplier discount alone never justifies overstock. (triggers: Each run; data sources: Contracts, milestones, deposits, product lifecycle; outputs and recipients: Decisions and reasons)
- **Policy store** — Thresholds, service targets, deposit requirements, credit limits and scenario assumptions are editable approved policy. AIPRO may propose changes with evidence but must not change policy itself. (triggers: On change proposal or approval; data sources: Owner and finance; outputs and recipients: Policy versions recorded per run)
- **Excel return workbook (Step 5 Return)** — An Excel workbook plus a short summary: recommended spend, changes, overbuying avoided, shortages and approvals needed. Sheets: Summary (requested vs recommended units and spend, cash needed by date, major changes, stockout warnings, assumptions; approval status starts 'Pending approval'); Order_Review (every uploaded line: SKU, requested and recommended quantity, decision, order date, ETA, supplier, price, currency, landed unit cost, line total, margin, reason code, evidence, confidence); Additional_Items (justified missing products with quantities and reasons, kept separate and never silently added to the order); Stock_Demand (usable and reserved stock, aged and dead value, firm demand, residual forecast, safety stock, dated inbound, lead time, coverage, projected shortage); Cash_Risk (deposits and allocations, AR/AP exceptions, payment schedule, credit and reserve headroom, logistics choices, scenario comparison); Sources_Exceptions (source record IDs and refresh times, mapping errors, missing fields, assumptions, calculation inputs, policy and model versions, approval and override history). Format: filters, frozen headers, explicit currencies and dates, a colour legend. BUY and ADJUST show quantities, WAIT and DO NOT ORDER show 0, HOLD stays blank. Totals reconcile, undefined ratios show N/A. Only fields permitted for the recipient are exported; hiding a confidential sheet is not enough. (triggers: End of each run; data sources: Engine output; outputs and recipients: The uploader and approvers, filtered by permission)
- **Approval and controlled draft PO (Step 6 Approve)** — An authorized person approves the exact items, quantities, supplier, terms and maximum spend; only then may a controlled draft PO be created. A change of price, quantity or terms needs fresh approval. Re-uploads and retries must not create duplicate POs. No unauthorized orders or payments. (triggers: Approver action; data sources: Workbook decision; outputs and recipients: Draft PO in SAP (stage 3 only); approval history)
- **Learning and forecasting** — Start with simple demand baselines. Compare seasonal models, intermittent-demand methods and ML models using rolling time splits with no future-data leakage, and use ML only where it improves tested outcomes. Retrain candidates on a schedule or when drift is detected. Store forecasts, orders, actual sales, lead times, stockouts, corrections and margin. Release improved versions after review, with rollback. Confidence reflects source coverage, freshness and backtest quality, not model self-assurance. With little history, use approved analogues or manual assumptions and lower confidence. (triggers: Scheduled or on drift; data sources: Stored history; outputs and recipients: Model versions; confidence per line)
- **Procurement knowledge base** — Procurement skills, SOPs, supplier guides and Incoterms references enter a versioned knowledge base after review, with source, owner, effective date and expiry. Knowledge retrieval and model training stay separate. Uploaded text cannot override approval rules or access rights. (triggers: On reviewed upload; data sources: Uploaded documents; outputs and recipients: Retrieval for explanations)
- **Business measures** — Track forecast error and bias against the baseline, fill rate and stockouts, aged and dead stock value, days of cover, gross margin return on inventory, landed-cost variance, supplier on-time performance, cash forecast error, override rate and analysis time. Guard against cutting stock so far that customer service worsens. (triggers: Ongoing; data sources: Stored runs and outcomes; outputs and recipients: Owner and managers (channel not specified))

## OMAI Virtual Warehouse Manager: IT team task and build specification

Build OMAI to coordinate EMJIEM warehouse and store stock (Garmin and Tanita, Primus equipment, parts, detergents, display units, LONDRY supplies) across configured locations. It tracks every movement, compares systems with physical evidence, schedules counts, assigns work and follows exceptions to closure. 'Self-running' means automated monitoring, scheduling and follow-up. Otabek and site staff still physically receive, scan, move, inspect and count, and OMAI reports what is verified, pending and unknown. OMAI plans and checks. It may handle routine steps under approved rules but cannot write off stock, approve credit, dispose of goods, discipline staff or make purchases. AIPRO owns purchase recommendations; CASH or a designated approver gives release authorization.

**Modules**

- **Roles and authority** — Otabek owns physical warehouse execution. Each store appoints a stock custodian. A separate checker verifies material variances. An authorized manager or accountant approves adjustments and write-offs within written limits. IT owns integrations, access and recovery. The owner receives exceptions that need an owner decision. People keep employment decisions. (triggers: Standing; data sources: Role assignments; outputs and recipients: Routing of tasks, approvals and escalations)
- **Receiving and put-away** — Match PO, packing list and delivery. Staff scan SKU, quantity, unit and serial or batch, and record shortages and damage. Only actual receipts are acknowledged, and physical arrival is kept separate from pending ERP posting. Bins are assigned by size, load, access, compatibility and demand; staff scan item and bin to confirm put-away. Damaged units are quarantined separately. (triggers: Every movement (receipt event); data sources: SAP open POs; scans; outputs and recipients: Movement register; shortage and damage exceptions)
- **Picking, dispatch and release control** — Pick the oldest suitable receipts first (FIFO), earliest expiry first for dated stock (FEFO). Damaged or expired units stay unavailable. Check the order and authorized release status, scan picked serials and quantities, and confirm packing and handover with proof. Digital release is blocked for missing authorization or conflicting stock; humans control physical access. (triggers: Each pick/dispatch; data sources: Orders, release approval (CASH/approver), scans; outputs and recipients: Dispatch record; blocks)
- **Transfers** — One linked transfer ID. Source dispatch is confirmed, transit is tracked, and destination receipt is required. Quantities move between these states once; the same units never show as available at both sites. Transit is a separate location state. (triggers: Each transfer; data sources: Transfer events; outputs and recipients: Movement register; incomplete-transfer exceptions)
- **Returns and service stock** — Each return is linked to its original document and serial and quarantined until inspected. Goods issued to technicians are recorded with expected return dates. Saleable availability is restored only after approved acceptance. Customer-owned and consignment goods are identified separately. (triggers: Each return/issue; data sources: Original documents, serials; outputs and recipients: Quarantine list; technician holdings)
- **Location awareness** — Tracks stock in warehouses, stores, showrooms, staging, transit and with technicians. (triggers: Continuous; data sources: SAP, Billz, the movement register; outputs and recipients: Location view)
- **Physical count procedure** — (1) Create a count ID with SKU and bin list, counter, checker and deadline; include zero-stock bins, unlisted goods, displays and pending movements. (2) Record the cutoff timestamp; freeze selected movements where supported, or use an approved movement log and reconcile every movement during the count. (3) A blind count form with no expected quantity; scan item, bin and serial or batch; an explicit 0 means counted empty, a blank means not counted. (4) Compare with the ledger at the same cutoff; investigate late postings, wrong units, duplicates and stock in other locations. (5) An independent recount for material differences and serial mismatches; both results kept, conflicting counts never averaged. (6) Record the cause and a corrective task; quantity corrections, write-offs or disposal need separate approval; stock is never changed just to make systems agree. (7) Verify the authorized posting succeeded, close the count, release the freeze, set the next count date, keep the full audit trail. Photos support evidence but do not prove total quantity. Goods, locations and movement events are linked following GS1 traceability principles. (triggers: Count calendar and extra triggers; data sources: Ledger at cutoff (SAP, 1C, Billz), scans; outputs and recipients: Variances, recount tasks, adjustment approval requests)
- **Counting calendar** — Group A (high value or risk): weekly, spread across daily capacity; recount after serial mismatches or repeated variances. Group B (medium): monthly; recount unexplained shortages, label errors or transfer issues. Group C (lower): quarterly; recount unexpected balances or location differences. All locations: a baseline before launch; full counts on the approved accounting calendar, plus custodian handover, incidents or system migration. Risk groups, capacity, tolerances and due dates are approved policy. The next date is calculated from the last accepted count, and original deadlines are kept when rescheduling. (triggers: Scheduler; data sources: Policy, last accepted counts; outputs and recipients: Count tasks)
- **Daily schedule (Asia/Tashkent pilot)** — Every movement: validate the event; staff scan the receipt, pick, transfer, return or dispatch and supply missing documents. Every 15 minutes: background check of sync, duplicates and stock holds; show source freshness; tasks only for actionable exceptions. 09:00: opening, access and hazard checks; Otabek confirms opening or reports an issue. 09:15: assign prioritized receiving, put-away, dispatch and count tasks; staff acknowledge or report a blocker. 11:00 and 15:00: review progress and transfer receipts; staff submit evidence, ask for help or propose a revised deadline. 17:30 and 17:50: reconcile movements, then confirm closing, locks, keys and unfinished handovers; one short digest. Each location's and employee's shift calendar is used. Routine reminders stay within shifts; defined urgent incidents go to the on-call person. (triggers: Clock, events; data sources: Shift calendars, task states; outputs and recipients: Otabek, site staff, the on-call person; one digest)
- **Task and escalation engine** — Each task has an ID, location, result, assignee, deadline, priority, evidence and accepting role. States: Assigned, In progress, Blocked, Submitted, Accepted, Recount required; overdue is a flag. Evidence is auto-accepted only under approved rules. Example: 'Otabek, count bin G01 by 11:00. Scan serials and submit.' Buttons: Start, Need help, Report problem, Submit. Expected quantities are hidden during blind counts. One reminder before the due time and one after. Unresolved work escalates to the responsible manager by an agreed deadline. OMAI asks for blockers and thanks verified completion. Urgent safety or unauthorized-release issues escalate immediately. Tasks and escalations go to MIYA. (triggers: Task lifecycle and clock; data sources: Task records; outputs and recipients: Assignees, accepting role, responsible manager, owner (for owner decisions))
- **Ageing and frozen stock** — Flags slow, dead, excess, expired, damaged and quarantined goods, with age and no-sale thresholds per category. Justified critical spares are kept. Stock facts, risks and clearance or replenishment proposals go to AIPRO. (triggers: Scheduled; data sources: Receipt dates, sales; outputs and recipients: AIPRO; permitted managers (aged value))
- **Safety checks** — Opening and closing checks; handling and storage inspections (aisles, storage, load limits, equipment, chemicals, access); repairs and hazard isolation under site procedures; qualified handlers for heavy equipment; closing and key handover verified; urgent hazards escalated. (triggers: 09:00 and 17:50; incidents; data sources: Site procedures; outputs and recipients: Otabek, on-call person, manager)
- **Stock data model and safeguards** — Fields: company, SKU, unit, barcode, location and bin, ownership, condition, serial or batch, receipt and expiry dates, quantity, allocation, source document, event time, posting time. Condition and allocation are stored separately, and reserved goods are a subset of stock, not extra units. Available = saleable on-hand minus active allocations, counted once. Planned arrivals stay separate from physical receipts. An event mirrored across platforms counts once. Each field has an authoritative source; conflicting balances create an exception, never an averaged number. Jobs and events use unique IDs, safe retries, alert queues and restart recovery. Data freshness is shown. Stale critical data stops unsupported digital releases, with a controlled manual fallback. Permissions are enforced in the database and tools, changes are logged, and approvals are bound to exact actions. (triggers: Always; data sources: All connectors; outputs and recipients: Internal)
- **Skills, knowledge and ML** — Reviewed, versioned skills for receiving, bin placement, picking, counting, returns and safe handling. Product manuals and site SOPs are retrieved with source dates; uploaded text cannot change access or approval policy. Rules come first. Candidate models are trained on verified movements, accepted counts and resolved causes to prioritize counts, detect unusual patterns and suggest slotting. They are compared with the baseline on later periods, false alarms are reviewed, improved versions released with rollback. An anomaly is a request to investigate, not proof of theft. Camera or sensor counting is optional and needs item-specific validation. OMAI never presents an inferred image count or an unverified staff reply as a completed physical inventory. (triggers: Scheduled retraining; data sources: Verified history; outputs and recipients: Count priorities, anomaly investigations, slotting suggestions)
- **Report and improve / owner dashboard** — Shows due tasks, discrepancies, late receipts, incomplete transfers, source freshness and recurring errors, and suggests better bin placement and count priorities. Unresolved exceptions stay visible even when staff report the task as finished. (triggers: Continuous; 17:50 digest; data sources: All of the above; outputs and recipients: Owner and managers (permission-filtered); Excel reports)
